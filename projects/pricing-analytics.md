## Problem

NYC taxi operators needed to understand how fare reforms affected pricing, demand, and revenue. The goal was a reproducible analytics pipeline — not a one-off notebook — covering cleaning, spatial filtering, fare–distance modelling, and price elasticity to support pricing decisions.

## Data sources

- NYC taxi trip data (`train.csv`) — fare amount, pickup/dropoff coordinates, passenger count, pickup datetime
- Manhattan geofence polygon for spatial filtering
- Pre/post fare reform boundary (September 2012) for segmented analysis

## Business impact (elasticity)

Estimated price elasticity **ε ≈ −0.91** (log-log demand model, R² ≈ 0.51 on daily aggregates). In plain terms: a **10% increase** in average price per km is associated with roughly a **9% decrease** in kilometres sold — inelastic but still material for revenue planning. Use with the online pricing simulation (median optimised price **~$3.02/km** on synthetic data) to explore fare bands before policy changes.

## Approach

- LightGBM replaces H2O GBM (no JVM, faster, pip-installable)
- Log-log demand model replaces linear (directly interpretable elasticity)
- Time-aware train/test split prevents leakage across the fare-reform boundary
- GeoPandas/Shapely polygon filter replaces manual apply loop
- Haversine distance replaces the custom Euclidean approximation
- All steps are functions — importable, testable, reproducible
- Matplotlib charts exported for portfolio and stakeholder reports

**Source code:** `content/code/pricing_analytics.py`

## Key findings

- OLS fare–distance slopes compared against official pre-reform ($1.243/km) and post-reform ($1.553/km) tariffs
- LightGBM improves prediction using hour, weekday, passenger count, and distance features
- Log-log demand model yields directly interpretable price elasticity (vs. misleading linear optimum)
- Online explore-then-exploit pricing algorithm queries real observed days instead of synthetic demand
- Hour-of-day patterns justify temporal features in fare prediction models
